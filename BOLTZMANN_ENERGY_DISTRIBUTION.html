Physical Principles of Ludwig Boltzmann’s Distribution (1877)

• Probability Density: P(E) = (1 / k_B T) · e^(-E / k_B T)
• Boltzmann Multiplicity: S = k_B · ln(Ω)
• Conservation Law: Total Energy E_total = ∑ (1/2 · m · v_i²) = Constant
  • Why Lower Energies Always Dominate: For a single particle to reach an extreme kinetic energy state ($E \gg k_B T$), many other surrounding particles must surrender their momentum and slow down. Because there are vastly fewer microscopic configurations (microstates $\Omega$) capable of concentrating energy into a few chosen particles, the probability decays exponentially.
  • Fast Relaxation of High-Energy Bursts: When you click "Inject 30 Fast Particles", watch the histogram. The sudden high-energy peak vanishes within seconds as fast particles undergo frequent collisions, dispersing their excess momentum and returning the ensemble to the classic Boltzmann curve.
