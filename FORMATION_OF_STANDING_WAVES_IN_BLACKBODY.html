Physics of Standing Wave Formation in Blackbody Radiation

  • Cavity Wall Boundary Conditions: In thermal equilibrium, radiation within a metallic blackbody enclosure is reflected repeatedly at conducting walls ($x = 0$ and $x = L$)[span_0](start_span)[span_0](end_span)[span_1](start_span)[span_1](end_span). The tangential electric field must be zero at both boundaries ($E_y(0, t) = 0$ and $E_y(L, t) = 0$), forcing the formation of stationary standing wave modes with discrete wavelengths $\lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n}$ ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$)[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span)[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span).
  • Field Phase Quadrature: Confined cavity radiation forms electromagnetic standing waves where the electric field $E_y(x, t) \propto \sin(kx)\cos(\omega t)$ and magnetic field $B_z(x, t) \propto \cos(kx)\sin(\omega t)$ oscillate $\frac{\pi}{2}$ radians ($90^\circ$) out of phase spatially and temporally[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span).
  • Thermal Oscillator Energy Exchange: Blackbody cavity walls contain atomic dipoles that absorb and emit thermal radiation[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span)[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span). The energy inside each standing wave mode continuously exchanges between electric potential energy ($U_E \propto E^2$) and magnetic kinetic energy ($U_B \propto B^2$), maintaining a classical average energy of $k_B T$ per mode[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span)[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span).
