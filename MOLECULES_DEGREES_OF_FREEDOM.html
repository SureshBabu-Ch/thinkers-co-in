Exact Degree of Freedom (DoF) Calculation & Equipartition

Loading calculation breakdown...
  • Translational Modes ($f_{\text{trans}} = 3$): Every molecule can translate independently along $x, y, z$ in 3D space, contributing $\frac{3}{2}RT$ to molar internal energy.
  • Rotational Modes ($f_{\text{rot}}$): Linear molecules have 2 rotational axes perpendicular to the internuclear axis ($I_{\parallel} \approx 0$). Non-linear molecules tumble around all 3 independent principal axes.
  • Vibrational Normal Modes ($N_{\text{vib}}$): Linear molecules possess $3N - 5$ normal modes (e.g., $3(3)-5 = 4$ for $\text{CO}_2$). Non-linear molecules possess $3N - 6$ normal modes (e.g., $3(3)-6 = 3$ for $\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
  • Energy Equipartition Multiplier ($\times 2$): In the classical limit, each normal vibrational mode contributes 2 quadratic terms (Kinetic: $\frac{1}{2}\mu v^2$ + Potential: $\frac{1}{2}kx^2$). Therefore, each active mode contributes $R$ to $C_v$ (or $1 k_B T$ to average molecular energy).
  • Quantum Freeze-Out: In rigid diatomics like $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$ at $300\text{ K}$, thermal energy ($k_B T \approx 207\text{ cm}^{-1}$) is far smaller than the vibrational gap ($\Delta E_{\text{vib}} > 1500\text{ cm}^{-1}$). Vibrations remain frozen ($v=0$) until high temperatures are reached.
